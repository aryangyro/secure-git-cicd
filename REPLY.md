# Discussion 4 reply — to Chioma Chineyenwa Iroka

Posted on Blackboard (MCSC-6002 Discussion 4) on Nov 19, 2026, as a reply to Chioma's post from Nov 18 about securing Git and CI/CD pipelines. Her post's incident-response angle was the part I replied to, since my own post focused on prevention — least privilege, branch protection, signed commits, OIDC tokens, and scanning.

> Hi Chioma, I really liked the second half of your post, the incident response part, because most of us (me included) focused on stopping the attack before it happens and kind of skipped what happens after something slips through. Your point about SolarWinds and MOVEit actually shows the problem with that — those were trusted, signed updates, so all the checks before deployment wouldnt have caught them. That makes me wonder how a team is supposed to spot a bad dependency once its already running in production. Do you think monitoring for weird behaviour after deployment matters more than the checks before it, or is it more about having a fast rollback plan ready? I also liked that you mentioned drills, because a response plan nobody has practiced probably falls apart in a real incident. One thing I am curious about is how a team decides when to roll back a compromised dependency and when to just patch it in place, since rolling back can break other things that depend on it. What would you do first if you found out a library you use every day was compromised?
